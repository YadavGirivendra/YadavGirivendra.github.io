About me

I'm a graduate researcher working at the intersection of atmospheric chemistry, numerical modeling, and high-performance computing. My current work focuses on adapting the Intervention Model for Air Pollution (InMAP) for the Indian subcontinent — running seasonal simulations across DJF, MAM, JJAS, and ON periods and benchmarking them against WRF-Chem and station observations.

A lot of my day-to-day is split between the Go source of InMAP, Python pipelines for evaluation and visualization, and the SLURM scheduler on our HPC cluster. I've recently built a Monte Carlo calibration pipeline that estimates a Jacobian via linear regression and uses bounded least-squares to tune model parameters against ~110 observation stations.

Beyond the technical work, I care about why this matters: India carries one of the highest particulate burdens in the world, and tools that translate emissions into exposure quickly and transparently are how we get from data to better policy.

Current research

Project · Ongoing

InMAP for India

Adapting the InMAP reduced-complexity model for seasonal air-quality simulation across the Indian subcontinent. Resolving the Mercator/LCC coordinate complexities and benchmarking against WRF-Chem.

Project · Ongoing

PAVITRA Sensitivity Suite

Evaluating five InMAP configurations across five PM₂.₅ species. Probing how dry-deposition velocity and vertical mixing parameters drive NMB and IOA against station data.

Method

Monte Carlo Calibration

A 500-sample parameter sweep run via SLURM, a regression-based Jacobian, and a bounded least-squares solve to tune four key parameters against ~110 PM₂.₅ observation stations.

Code · InMAP fork

Split Dry Deposition

Separating primary and secondary particle dry-deposition pathways in the InMAP Go codebase — touching framework, preproc, chem, and vargrid — to enable independent parameter tuning.
